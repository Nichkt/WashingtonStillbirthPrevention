Washington should teach every pregnant parent to track their baby's movements

1 in 175

U.S. births is a stillbirth. Washington loses about 360 to 440 babies a year at 20 weeks or later.

About 1 in 4

stillbirths is caused by placental disease. Those babies often move less in the days or weeks before.

32%

drop in Iowa's stillbirth rate in the first ten years of Count the Kicks, which teaches parents to track movement.

If you're pregnant

  1. Starting around 28 weeks, or earlier if your provider tells you to, count every day at a time when your baby is usually active.
  2. Time how long it takes to feel 10 movements. Kicks, rolls, jabs and swishes all count.
  3. Write it down. After a few days you'll know what's normal for your baby.

Call your provider or go to labor and delivery right away if it takes a lot longer than usual to feel 10 movements, if your baby is moving less, or if you feel a change in how your baby moves. Don't wait until the next day, and don't try cold water or sugary drinks first. Babies should keep moving all the way into labor.

The free Count the Kicks app for iPhone and Android can time your counts and remind you each day.
